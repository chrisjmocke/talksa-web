LETS TALK SA (Pty) Ltd. Prepared under section 51 of the Promotion of Access to Information Act, 2000 (PAIA). Last updated 2 October 2026.

1. About this manual

PAIA gives everyone the right to ask for records held by a private body, where those records are needed to exercise or protect a right. This manual explains what records LETS TALK SA (Pty) Ltd keeps, how to ask for them, and how we handle personal information under the Protection of Personal Information Act, 2013 (POPIA).

3. The Information Regulator's guide

The Information Regulator publishes a guide on how to use PAIA and POPIA, including how to make a request and what to do if it is refused. It is available in all official languages on the Regulator's website, inforegulator.org.za, or from the Regulator directly.

We keep records required by, among others, the Companies Act 71 of 2008, the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962, the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, the Value-Added Tax Act 89 of 1991 (if registered), the Consumer Protection Act 68 of 2008, the Electronic Communications and Transactions Act 25 of 2002, and POPIA.

6. Records we hold

  • Company records: registration documents, memorandum of incorporation, director records, resolutions.
  • Financial and tax records: bank statements, invoices, accounting records, tax returns.
  • Customer records: user accounts, credit balances, purchase and subscription records, and correspondence with customers.
  • Service provider records: agreements and invoices from the providers that run the app.
  • Technical records: the app's source code, server configuration and server logs.
  • Intellectual property: the Let's Talk name, logo, app and website content.

7. How we process personal information

Purpose

To provide translation, transcription and speech, run user accounts and credit balances, take payments, prevent abuse of the free trial, meet our legal duties, and answer enquiries.

Who we hold information about, and what

  • App users: email address, name (if signing in with Google), a device identifier, credit balance, purchase records, and the text and voice notes sent for translation.
  • Customers who pay: email address and Paystack payment references. We do not receive card details.
  • Service providers: company names, contact people, contact and banking details.

Who receives it

Google (Firebase, Google Cloud, Gemini and Text-to-Speech), Narakeet and Paystack, each only for its part in running the app. We may also disclose information where the law requires it, for example to the South African Revenue Service.

Information sent outside South Africa

Our server runs in South Africa, but some providers process information in other countries. We use providers bound by data protection terms that protect information to a standard similar to POPIA.

How we protect it

Information is encrypted while being sent and stored with Google Cloud. Access is limited: each user can only read their own account, and balances and payments can only be changed by our server. Our full Privacy Policy is at www.talksa.co.za/privacy.html.

8. How to request a record

  1. Complete Form 2 (Request for Access to Record), available from the Information Regulator's website.
  2. Send it to our Information Officer at letstalktranslate@gmail.com or the postal address above.
  3. Give enough detail for us to find the record, say how you want to receive it, and explain which right you need it for.
  4. If you ask on someone else's behalf, include proof that you may act for them.

We will decide within 30 days and tell you in writing. If we need more time, we may extend this once by up to 30 days and will tell you why.

People asking for their own personal information can simply email us; see the Privacy Policy.

9. Fees

A request fee and access fees for copying and preparing records may apply, as set out in the PAIA regulations. No request fee is payable when you ask for your own personal information. We will tell you about any fee before preparing the record.

10. Refusal and complaints

We may refuse a request only on the grounds set out in Chapter 4 of Part 3 of PAIA, for example to protect another person's privacy, commercial information or confidential information. If your request is refused, or you are unhappy with how it was handled, you may complain to the Information Regulator within 180 days, or apply to court.
